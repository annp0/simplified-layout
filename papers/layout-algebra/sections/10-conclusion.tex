\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

CuTe's layout algebra describes how tiles of tensors map onto threads and
memory, but its operations are guarded by at least six interacting
admissibility conditions, none of them visible in its API, and where they
fail the result can be wrong without a diagnostic. We restrict
what a layout can be instead. Every layout declares the space of its input and
of its output, and the space it maps into fixes what it must satisfy: a dense
bijection into logical or thread-value indices, and alias-freedom, up to
declared replicas, into physical offsets. With that restriction composition
needs a single condition, the complement is no longer needed as an operation,
and division, repetition, interleaving, broadcast and inversion are given by
direct formulas. Whether a composite is again a layout is decided rather than
searched for: one pass checks separability and a linear scan recognizes each
coordinate, and codegen emits the coarsest form or, failing that, the
composite. A thread's slice is taken last, by restriction. CuTe's operations
are reproduced on its documented examples, the atoms and usage patterns of
CUTLASS are rederived, and the decision procedure is mechanized in Coq.

The algebra is built for a GPU DSL in which each warp is a SIMD program and
warps communicate through pipes, in the sense of Unix pipes, lowered to
mbarriers at compile time. The layouts of this paper describe how the lanes of
a warp access a tile. The immediate next step is the compiler that connects
the two: one that consumes these layouts and emits GPU code, so that kernels
built from the algebra alone can be measured against their CuTe counterparts,
in run time and in the code generated.
